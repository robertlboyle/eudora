Eudora on your phone

The iPhone and Android versions are in testing and not yet in the App Store or Google Play.

Eudora on the phone is made for the phone: big buttons, one screen at a time, light or dark with the phone. Like the desktop, it keeps its own library on the phone, downloaded from your mail servers, so search works on everything it holds, even offline.

Finding your way

  • The tab bar at the bottom starts as Mail, Calendar, To Do, and More. Change it in More ▸ Settings ▸ Tab bar (Search, Contacts, Views, and Mailboxes can go there too).
  • Mail opens on the filtered Inbox across every account. Mailboxes, accounts, and Views are a tap away.
  • Pull down to check for mail.
  • Long-press a message to choose several, then Delete, Move, Flag, Spam, or Forward.
  • Reading a message, swipe left for the next and right for the one before. Rows in the list have no swipe actions, so nothing is deleted by a stray thumb.

What it does

Read, search, write, send, reply, forward, delete, move, flag, Spam and Not Spam, To Do, Views, and Settings. The calendar and contacts can be read. Rules and the spam filter run on your computer; the moves they make reach the phone through the server, and the phone's own moves reach your computer the same way.

Setting up the phone

Add accounts in More ▸ Accounts: Microsoft 365 or Outlook.com, Gmail or Google Workspace, or Another mail account (IMAP with its outgoing server; POP3 isn't offered on the phone). Sign-in pages open over Eudora.

How much mail to keep

Before the first download, the phone asks how much mail to keep:

  • the last 30 days, 90 days, or a year; Another period… in days, weeks, months, or years; or Everything;
  • or Up to a size… (1, 5, 20, or 50 GB, or any): the newest mail first, until the phone's library takes up that much.

The newest mail comes first. As time passes, Eudora trims the phone's copy of older mail to stay within your choice; the servers keep it all. Mail kept only on the phone, flagged messages, and messages with a change still waiting are never trimmed, and with a size limit the last week always stays. Change the choice in More ▸ Settings ▸ Mail on this phone.

The Online Archive isn't downloaded to phones.

Attachments, new mail, and notifications on the phone

Tap an attachment for Open (on the iPhone, Apple's viewer; on Android, the phone's app for that kind of file) or Share or Save… (Save to Files, another app, AirDrop, Print). When writing, Attach picks photos, files, or the camera; × takes a file off again.

The phone checks for mail while Eudora is open, and in the background as often as the phone allows (about every 15 minutes at best). Eudora asks once whether it may send notifications: one per check ("3 new messages", with the newest's sender and subject), and the number on its icon is your unread Inbox mail.

Backups and sync on the phone

More ▸ Back Up & Restore:

  • Restore a backup from any computer to fill the phone with your whole library. Restoring brings everything in the backup.
  • Back Up Now makes a backup of the phone, with or without a password (with one, your mail-server passwords go in it). See the warning in Restoring a backup about phone backups.

On the iPhone, backups are in the Files app under On My iPhone ▸ Eudora (and in the Finder over USB). Copy a computer's backup there and it's listed for Restore.

On Android, Choose a Backup File… picks a backup from anywhere the phone can reach (Downloads, a USB stick, a cloud drive; several files for a backup in parts), and Save a Copy… puts a phone backup wherever you choose. Backups Eudora makes itself are kept in its own folder, which is deleted if Eudora is uninstalled, so save a copy elsewhere.

Sync: More ▸ Settings ▸ Sync with your computers ▸ Join with the recovery phrase, and your labels, signatures, Views, and other settings come from your computers. See Sync between your computers.
