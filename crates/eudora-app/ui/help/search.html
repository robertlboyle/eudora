Searching your mail

Type in the search box at the top of the window (Edit ▸ Find Messages…, Ctrl+F) and press Enter. Eudora searches every account, every folder, and every year at once (except mailboxes you left out of search): subjects, senders and recipients, the text of messages, the names of attachments, and the text inside PDF attachments of receipts and payments.

Results come newest first. Click a column heading to sort them another way. You can work with search results as with any list: read, reply, move, delete, or run the spam filter or your rules on them.

The last word you type also matches longer words starting with it: walkerfi finds walkerfield.example.

When nothing matches, Eudora says No results. When your search has a mistake, Eudora repairs what it safely can, says what it did, and offers corrected searches under Try:. Click one to run it.

To build a search from several conditions without typing search words, use Advanced Search, the button beside the search box. To find words in the message you're reading or writing, use Find Text.

Advanced Search (Find Messages)

Advanced Search builds a search from several conditions, so you don't need to know any search words. It's classic Eudora's Find Messages window, brought up to date.

Opening it

  • Click the sliders button beside the search box at the top of the window.
  • Or choose Edit ▸ Advanced Search…. In the Outlook, Thunderbird, Gmail, and Yahoo looks it's on the Edit menu too.
  • In Eudora Classic it is Edit ▸ Find ▸ Find Messages… (Ctrl+F), where classic Eudora had it. In the other looks Ctrl+F goes to the search box.

Building a search

  1. Each row is one condition: a field, a test, and a value. For example: From · contains · ann@example.com.
  2. Click + at the end of a row to add a condition after it (it starts like the row before), or − to remove that row. More adds a row at the end and Fewer removes the last one, as in classic Eudora.
  3. Choose Match all conditions (every row must hold) or Match any condition (one is enough).
  4. Under Search in, keep Every account, or choose Only these accounts and tick the ones to search.
  5. Click Search or press Enter. The results show in the message list, like any search. Open Advanced Search again to change the conditions: it remembers them.

Words match whole words, as in the search box: "contains report" finds "report" in the subject or text, not "deportation". "Starts with" matches the start of words. "Ends with" for an address finds that domain anywhere in the address.

The search it makes

Below the conditions, Eudora shows the search they make, in the same words the search box takes, so you can see how to type it next time. For example, From contains ann, Status is Unread, and Date is within last week with Match all make from:ann is:unread received:"last week".

You can change the search there by hand, for instance to add parentheses for a mix of "and" and "or": (from:ann OR from:bob) is:unread. Once you do, the conditions above no longer make it, and Eudora says so: Make It from the Conditions Again puts back what the rows make.

Clear starts again with one empty condition. Help opens this page.

With the keyboard and a screen reader

Every control has a name a screen reader reads, such as "Condition 2: field". Tab moves through a row's field, test, value, +, and −; arrow keys choose in a list; Enter in a value searches; Esc closes the window. Adding or removing a condition is announced ("Condition 3 added: From contains. 3 conditions."), and the keyboard moves to the new row's value, or to the row before the one removed.

The words it uses are listed in Search words and operators. Mailboxes you left out of search are left out here too.

Headers

Any word followed by a colon that isn't one of the words above searches the header of that name: x-mailer:eudora, list-id:* (has the header at all), -list-id:* (doesn't). While Eudora is still indexing headers of older mail, the results say how much it covers so far.

These are the words Outlook and Gmail use, so what you know from them works here too.

Find Text in a message

Edit ▸ Find Text… (Ctrl+Alt+F; in Classic mode Ctrl+Shift+F) finds words in the message you're writing, or else in the one you're reading, and highlights each. F3 finds the next, Shift+F3 the one before.

Leaving mailboxes out of search

Each row on the left (Accounts, Shared Mailboxes, every account, every folder) has a small magnifying glass beside its icon. Click it to leave that mailbox out of search: the magnifying glass is then struck through in red. Click it again to search it again.

A switch applies to everything under it, and a switch lower down wins. So you can leave out all of Shared Mailboxes, then switch one shared mailbox back on, or leave out a whole account and search just its Inbox.

Nothing is deleted or taken out of the index: the mail is still kept and still found the moment you search it again. A message that's also in a folder that is searched still shows.

While anything is left out, a magnifying glass with ! appears beside the search box. It's gray while searches leave those mailboxes out; click it to Include all mailboxes (it turns bold and colored), and again to leave them out. When a search finds more in the mailboxes left out, the results say how many, with Include them.
