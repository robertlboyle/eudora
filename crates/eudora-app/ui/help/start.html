Welcome to Eudora

Eudora is a free mail program for Windows and the Mac, with phone versions on the way. It is built for very large mailboxes: millions of messages and decades of mail, with searches and folders that open at once.

All of your mail goes into one library on your computer: Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com, Gmail, any IMAP or POP3 account, and old mail you import (classic Eudora folders, mbox files, .eml files). You get one search, one set of rules, and one window over all of it.

Your mail servers stay the master copy. Eudora keeps a full copy on your computer, so it works without a connection. When you move, delete, flag, or read a message, Eudora makes the same change on the server as soon as it can.

How this manual is organized

  • Getting started: adding your accounts, bringing in old mail, and finding your way around the window.
  • Tasks: reading, searching, writing, organizing, sorting, the calendar, contacts, To Do, and keeping things running.
  • Reference: every menu, keyboard shortcut, window, and setting.
  • Troubleshooting: what to do when something doesn't work as expected.
  • Index: every topic A to Z. The search box at the top finds any word in the manual.

Open this manual at any time with Help ▸ Eudora Help, F1, or on the Mac ⌘+?. Press Ctrl+F (on the Mac ⌘+F) or / to jump to the search box.

Windows and the Mac

Eudora works the same way on both. On the Mac, the ⌘ (Command) key takes the place of Ctrl in every shortcut, and ⌥ (Option) takes the place of Alt. This manual writes the Windows keys.

The first time you start Eudora

The first time Eudora starts, it makes an empty library and shows the Welcome window. Choose how to bring in your mail:

  • Microsoft 365 or Outlook.com: sign in with Microsoft in your browser. See Adding a Microsoft account.
  • Any other mail account: IMAP or POP3 for Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud, or your company's or provider's mail server. See Adding an IMAP or POP3 account.
  • Old mail on this computer: classic Eudora folders, mbox, or .eml files. See Importing old mail.
  • Restore from a backup: everything from a Eudora backup made on this or another computer. See Restoring a backup.

Before adding an account, choose how much mail to keep on this computer with the slider in the Welcome window: Everything, or the last 1 month to 20 years. On a tablet or a computer with a small drive, a shorter period keeps Eudora from downloading years of mail and filling the disk: only that period is downloaded, newest first. Your mail stays on its servers either way, and you can change the period later to any length (Keeping only recent mail on this computer).

You can choose Later and add accounts at any time in Settings ▸ Accounts.

Where your mail is kept

The library lives in your user folder:

Windows%LOCALAPPDATA%\Eudora\Library
Mac~/Library/Application Support/Eudora/Library
Linux~/.local/share/eudora/library

It holds the original bytes of every message, a database of what's in each folder, and the search indexes. Attachments you open or save go into its Attachments folder, in a folder for each year.

Never put the library in Dropbox, OneDrive, Google Drive, iCloud Drive, or any other syncing folder. Those programs lock files while Eudora is writing them, which can damage the database. Eudora refuses to restore a backup into such a folder. To move mail between computers, use Back Up Eudora or sync between your computers.

Large mailboxes take a while to come down the first time. Eudora downloads your newest mail first, so you can start working at once, and fills in older mail behind it. The status bar at the bottom of the window shows real numbers as it goes.

Adding a Microsoft account

Eudora works with Microsoft 365 (work or school accounts) and with personal Outlook.com, Hotmail, and Live accounts.

  1. Choose Settings ▸ Accounts ▸ Add a Microsoft account… (or Microsoft 365 or Outlook.com in the Welcome window).
  2. Your browser opens Microsoft's sign-in page. Sign in as usual.
  3. Microsoft asks whether Eudora may use your mail, calendar, contacts, and tasks. Choose Accept. Some organizations require their administrator to approve Eudora first.
  4. Your browser says you can close it, and the mail starts coming in.

You can add as many Microsoft accounts as you have. The first one you add is your default account: it carries your To Do list and, unless you choose otherwise, your synced settings.

Eudora never sees your Microsoft password. It keeps only the sign-in Microsoft gives it, in Windows Credential Manager or the Mac's Keychain.

Your Online Archive

Many Microsoft 365 mailboxes have an Online Archive holding older mail. To bring it down too, turn on Include the Online Archive in Settings ▸ Downloading and sync. Its folders join your mailbox's folders, so you can manage the whole mailbox as one. See The Online Archive.

Shared mailboxes

To add shared mailboxes (or other people's mailboxes you were given access to), choose Find shared mailboxes… in the Accounts settings and tick the ones you want. See Shared mailboxes.

Signing in again

When Eudora gains a new ability that needs Microsoft's permission, or when your organization resets sign-ins, choose Sign in to Microsoft 365 again… in the Accounts settings. Eudora shows there what it may do on the server.

Adding a Google account

Choose Settings ▸ Accounts ▸ Add a Google account…. Your browser opens Google's sign-in page; sign in and allow Eudora. Eudora then gets your mail, sending, labels, contacts, and calendars through Google's own connection.

In Gmail, a message can carry several labels. In Eudora, each label is a folder, and a message with several labels appears in each of those folders while being stored only once. Moving a message in Eudora changes its labels in Gmail:

  • Moving to another folder takes the old label off and puts the new one on.
  • Deleting moves it to Gmail's Trash. Eudora's Empty Trash leaves Gmail's Trash alone; Gmail empties it after 30 days.
  • Moving to Spam also takes it out of the Inbox, as Gmail's Report spam does.
  • A folder you make in Eudora becomes a Gmail label. Use "/" in the name to nest it.

Changes you make in Gmail itself, on your phone or the web, come into Eudora within a minute or so.

You can also add Gmail as an IMAP account with a Google app password. Signing in with Google is better: it brings in contacts and calendars too, and needs no app password.

Adding an IMAP or POP3 account

Choose Settings ▸ Accounts ▸ Add another account… (also Tools ▸ Add Account…).

  1. Type your email address. Eudora looks up who hosts it. A Microsoft or Google address is sent to Sign in with Microsoft or Sign in with Google instead, because those services no longer accept passwords over IMAP.
  2. Choose IMAP or POP3 for incoming mail. IMAP keeps your folders on the server and is almost always the better choice. POP3 is for old mailboxes that only offer it. This choice can't be changed after the account is saved.
  3. Check the servers Eudora filled in (it guesses imap., pop., or smtp. and your domain, or a provider's own names), or type the exact server names and ports your provider gives: what you type is always kept as you typed it; switching between IMAP and POP3, or changing the security, never replaces a server name or port you entered. Then type your user name and password. Many providers (Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud) need an app password made on their website rather than your usual password.
  4. Choose Test. Eudora signs in to both servers and lists your folders without saving anything or sending any mail.
  5. Choose Save. The account's mail starts coming in at once.

Ports and security

Each server has a Security choice (SSL/TLS, STARTTLS, or None) and a port. Until you type a port, changing the security moves it to the standard one (IMAP 993 / 143, POP3 995 / 110, SMTP 465 with SSL/TLS or 587 with STARTTLS); a port you typed stays. Beside a port that isn't the standard one, standard: 995 (for example) puts the standard one back.

Encryption level, for each server:

  • High (the usual): TLS 1.3 and TLS 1.2 with today's strong ciphers only.
  • Strict: TLS 1.3 only.
  • Compatible (Windows): High first, and only if the server can't agree on that, the system's own older encryption. Use it only for an old server that needs it.

Server certificates are always checked, whatever the level.

Options for each account

  • Make Eudora's changes on the server too (IMAP, on by default): moves, read marks, labels, and deletes are made on the server. Turn it off to keep this account's mail on this computer only, apart from the server's (what that means).
  • Delete from server after N days and Skip big messages over N MB (POP3). Eudora never deletes from a POP3 server unless you choose this.
  • Check every N minutes: how often this account is checked. See Checking for new mail.

Passwords are kept in Windows Credential Manager or the Mac's Keychain, never in the library.

Adding contacts and calendars (CardDAV / CalDAV)

Microsoft 365, Outlook.com, and Google bring their contacts and calendars with their accounts. Most other services keep them on a CardDAV (contacts) and CalDAV (calendars) server: iCloud, Fastmail, Nextcloud, Yahoo, and many providers' and companies' servers. Eudora reads them and sends your changes back.

  1. Choose Settings ▸ Accounts ▸ Add contacts and calendars (CardDAV / CalDAV)….
  2. Type your email address and password. iCloud and Fastmail need an app password made on their website. Left empty, the password of your IMAP account with the same address is used, which is right for most providers' own servers.
  3. Choose Find and add. Eudora finds the server from your address and says which address books and calendars it found. If it can't find it, type the server your provider's help names in Server, and your User name if it isn't your address. cPanel hosting: Eudora also looks on port 2080 of your mail server, where cPanel keeps contacts and calendars; if your mail server has another name, type it in Server (without a port).

Some servers keep only calendars, or only contacts; Eudora takes what the server has. The account is listed on the Accounts page with Remove…, which takes its contacts and calendars out of Eudora on this computer and leaves them on the server.

What goes both ways

  • Contacts: read every hour; a contact you add, change, or delete in the Address Book is changed on the server. Fields Eudora doesn't edit (photos, other programs' own fields) are kept as they are.
  • Calendars: the near weeks every 15 minutes, everything once a day; each occurrence of a repeating event is its own event (when a server sends only the repeat rule, as cPanel's does, Eudora works out the occurrences itself, in the event's own time zone). Appointments you make, change, or delete go to the server; deleting one occurrence of a repeating event removes just that one. Answering an invitation sets your answer on the server, which tells the organizer.
  • Changing one occurrence of a repeating event isn't done on these servers yet: change the whole series, or that occurrence in the server's own calendar.
  • When the server has a newer copy than the one you changed (changed on your phone meanwhile), Eudora doesn't overwrite it: your change waits, says so, and the server's copy comes in at the next check. Eudora asks the server for its copy's version just before writing, so this holds on servers that don't check versions themselves (cPanel's).

Passwords are kept in Windows Credential Manager or the Mac's Keychain, never in the library. Eudora talks to these servers only over https.

Keeping an account's changes on this computer only

Eudora normally makes your changes on the server too: a message you move, read, or delete, an event you change, a contact you edit. Your phone, the web, and other mail programs then see them. Some people want the opposite: a copy in Eudora that's independent of the server's, where what they do in Eudora stays in Eudora. You can choose that for each account, separately for mail, the calendar (with To Do), and contacts.

When you're asked

When you add an account (Microsoft, Google, IMAP, or contacts and calendars from a CardDAV / CalDAV server), Eudora asks once, in Changes on the server, with a switch for each of the account's services. Keep everything in step with the server leaves things as they've always been. A POP3 account has no mail switch: POP3 has no folders, flags, or moves to write back.

To change it later: Settings ▸ Accounts, then the account's Changes on the server… button (it reads Kept here only: … while something is kept local). An IMAP account's own window has the mail switch too, as Make Eudora's changes on the server too.

What "on this computer only" means

  • Mail: moves, deletes, read, flagged, and replied marks, labels, Empty Trash, the spam filter's and your rules' moves, and new, renamed, or deleted mailboxes are made in Eudora only. New mail still comes in. What changes on the server (read on your phone, moved or deleted on the web, a folder renamed in Outlook) doesn't change Eudora's copy, and mail deleted on the server stays in Eudora. Mail you moved or deleted in Eudora isn't downloaded again. Sending mail still sends, and its copy still goes to the server's Sent folder.
  • Calendar and To Do: new, changed, and deleted events, your answers to invitations, and tickets added to trips stay in Eudora: no invitations, answers, or cancellations are sent. New events still come in; an event already in Eudora keeps your version, an event deleted on the server stays, and one you deleted doesn't come back. Microsoft To Do goes with the calendar switch of your first Microsoft account.
  • Contacts: new, changed, merged, moved, and deleted contacts stay in Eudora. New contacts still come in; contacts already in Eudora keep your version, those deleted on the server stay, and those you deleted don't come back.

Turning it off again

Turning a service local drops the changes that were still waiting for the server (they stay in Eudora). Turning it back on sends only the changes you make from then on: nothing you did while it was local is ever sent to the server. Where Eudora's copy and the server's differ, the server's read marks, labels, events, and contacts may show in Eudora again as it reads them, and items you deleted while local can come back from the server.

The choice belongs to this computer: another computer with the same account keeps its own. Scripts can read and change it too: eudora-cli local-only, and the API's v1.accounts.local_only and v1.accounts.local_only_set (more).

Importing old mail

Choose File ▸ Import… (or Old mail on this computer in the Welcome window). You can bring in:

  • A classic Eudora folder (the one holding eudora.ini and your .mbx mailboxes), with its folder tree.
  • .mbx and mbox files, one or many.
  • .eml files, or folders of them.

Choose which account the mail belongs to and which folder it goes into. A classic Eudora folder becomes its owner's account, named by the return address in its settings, with its personalities.

When the address already has an account in Eudora, Eudora asks whether to merge the mail into it or keep it apart as an account of its own.

Nothing is lost, nothing is doubled

  • A message that can't be read properly is still kept whole, searchable as text, and listed in the import report.
  • If the same message is already in Eudora (say, old Eudora mail you once copied into Exchange), Eudora recognizes it by its Message-ID and keeps one message with both places recorded. It shows the most original copy: your own files first, then a server's copy.

Big mailboxes and backup files

  • A mailbox of any size imports whole. Classic Eudora itself couldn't read past 2 GB of a mailbox (it kept each message's place as a 32-bit number), but Eudora reads every message in the file, however big it grew.
  • Files named like In.mbx.001, In.mbx.002, or Out.toc.001 are left out. They aren't more mail: classic Eudora copies In and Out aside before each Compact (two copies, unless its InOutBackups setting says otherwise). Bringing them in would add old copies of those mailboxes, including mail deleted since. The import report lists each one it left out and why; to bring one in anyway, copy it to a name ending in .mbx and import that file.

Importing only adds

An import copies mail into Eudora's library and never changes the files it reads. Your classic Eudora folder, .mbx files, and .eml files stay exactly as they were, and you can go on using them in classic Eudora.

The import only ever adds to Eudora's library. It never moves or deletes anything already there. So:

  • Importing the same folder again brings in only what's new. Messages already in Eudora are recognized and skipped. Use this to pick up mail that arrived in classic Eudora after your first import.
  • Changes made in classic Eudora after an import aren't carried over. Moving a message to another mailbox there, or deleting it, doesn't move or delete Eudora's copy. Importing again adds the moved message's new place in Eudora too, and the deleted one stays in Eudora. Make those changes in Eudora instead.
  • Once imported, the two copies are independent: Eudora keeps its own copy of every message, and nothing it does reaches the old files.

Importing while classic Eudora is open

You can import from a classic Eudora that's still running: Eudora only reads its files and doesn't stop classic Eudora from using them. While the import runs, though:

  • Compacting can fail in classic Eudora. Windows doesn't let one program shrink a file another program is reading, so Compact Mailboxes or deleting a mailbox may fail for the mailbox being imported at that moment. Try again once the import has finished.
  • A mailbox is read as it was at that moment. Mail that arrives in a mailbox Eudora has already read isn't in this import. Import again later to get it. A mailbox compacted while Eudora is reading it can make Eudora skip or misread messages near the change. Nothing is thrown away (unreadable mail is kept whole and listed in the import report), but a second import afterwards is the way to be sure.

The safest way is to close classic Eudora while importing, or at least not to compact, move, or delete mail in it until Eudora says the import is done.

Old attachments

Classic Eudora saved attachments as separate files. After importing, choose Tools ▸ Find Old Attachments… to have Eudora look for them in the classic folder (and any folders you add, such as an old backup drive). Files found are stored in the library, so they travel with your backups. Ones that can't be found show as "(not found)" with their old path.

After importing classic Eudora mail, Eudora offers once to switch to Eudora Classic, which looks and works like the Eudora you know.

You can also open a single message file without importing it: see Opening .eml and .ics files.

The main window

The main window has four parts:

  • The toolbar at the top: Check Mail, New, Reply, Reply All, Forward, Transfer, Delete, Contacts, the search box, the look menu, and the light / dark switch. When the window is narrow the buttons drop their labels.
  • The sidebar on the left: your mailboxes and folders, the Views, Calendar, Contacts, and To Do. See The sidebar. Tools ▸ Mailboxes shows or hides it.
  • The message list: the messages in what you've chosen. See The message list.
  • The reading pane: the selected message. Drag the bar between the list and the message to give either more room. See Reading a message.

The status bar along the bottom shows what Eudora is doing in the background (checking mail, sorting, sending) with real counts, and buttons to pause or stop it. Click it to open Activity.

Eudora opens where you left it: the same mailbox, the same message, at once, and then brings the list up to date.

Closing the main window asks first, in case you meant to close a message. File ▸ Exit (Ctrl+Q) doesn't ask.

Light and dark

The sun / moon button beside the look menu switches between light and dark. Right-click it to follow the system's setting again. In dark mode, messages are shown dark too: their own colors are turned around, as Outlook does. Printing always uses light colors.

Organizing by folders or labels

Settings ▸ General ▸ Organize mail by: Folders shows each account with its folder tree; Labels shows one list of folder names across every account, as Gmail does (for example, one "Inbox" holding every account's inbox).
