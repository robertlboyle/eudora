Writing a new message

Choose Message ▸ New Message (Ctrl+N) or New on the toolbar. A message window opens in the main window's look.

  1. Choose who it's From (see Sending from another address).
  2. Type the people in To, Cc, and Bcc. Separate them with commas, semicolons, or spaces. Eudora suggests addresses as you type (see Address completion).
  3. Type the Subject and the message.
  4. Choose Send (Ctrl+E, Message ▸ Send Immediately).

The window closes at once; the message goes into the Out mailbox and is sent in the background. The status bar of the main window says when it went. A message that can't be sent (no connection, a server refusing it) waits in Out and is tried again.

The people in Bcc receive the message, but their addresses aren't in what's sent.

Replying, forwarding, redirecting

ReplyCtrl+RTo the sender (or the address they asked replies to go to), with the original quoted under "At 05:29 PM 12/31/1999, Ann Smith wrote:". The reply comes from the address the message was sent to.
Reply to AllCtrl+Shift+RTo the sender and everyone else on the message except you; Cc stays Cc.
Forward"Fwd:" with the original and its attachments. Untick any attachment you don't want to send along. With several messages selected, each is attached whole to one message.
RedirectSends the message on as it is, from you "by way of" the original sender; replies go to the original sender.
Send AgainA new message with the same recipients, subject, text, and attachments, for a message of yours that needs sending again.

Once sent, the original is marked replied, forwarded, or redirected, on the server too (Outlook and your phone show it). Mail you answer in Outlook, on your phone, or in webmail is marked in Eudora too: Microsoft 365 is asked about its newest messages every few minutes, an IMAP server about each folder it checks. (Gmail keeps no such mark.)

How replies quote the original

Settings ▸ Mail ▸ Replies chooses one of two ways:

  • Write above the original (the usual way): the original is quoted below, with a line down its side, and the cursor waits above it. Your signature goes between your words and the original.
  • Classic Eudora: the reply is plain text, with "> " before each line of the original (">> " for lines it had quoted already, long lines wrapped first), and you write below it (bottom posting). The cursor waits under the quote, and your signature goes at the end.

Just this once the other way: hold Shift or Ctrl while you click Reply or Reply to All (on the toolbar, in the right-click menu, or on the message). The keyboard shortcuts always use the way chosen in Settings.

A classic reply is plain text. Switch it to HTML with the message window's HTML / Plain Text switch if you want to format it: the ">" lines stay as they are.

In Settings ▸ General ▸ Look you can map Ctrl+R to Reply to All, as classic Eudora allowed.

In the Gmail-style look, replies and forwards are written in the message's place rather than in a window.

Sending from another address

Click the From line to choose any address you can send from: each account's address and each shared mailbox. The message is sent through the account that owns the address: Microsoft 365 or Gmail through its own connection, other accounts through their outgoing (SMTP) server. A copy goes into that account's Sent folder.

Classic Eudora personalities are listed too, grayed out, when no account can send as them.

File ▸ Accounts… (Classic: Tools ▸ Personalities…) opens Settings at its Accounts page, where each account's address and name are set.

Address completion

After two letters in To, Cc, or Bcc, Eudora suggests people. Use ↑ / ↓, then Enter or Tab to pick one; Esc closes the list. Suggestions come from:

  1. people you've written to (most often first), and people who've written to you;
  2. your Address Book;
  3. your organization's Microsoft 365 directory, added a moment later when it answers.

Settings ▸ General ▸ Address completion turns the first two sources on or off. Your own addresses are never suggested.

Correcting a name: if a suggestion has someone's name wrong (say "Mikee Jones <mike@jones.com>"), fix it in To, Cc, or Bcc ("Mike Jones <mike@jones.com>") and send the message. From then on Eudora suggests the name as you wrote it. The correction stays even when Eudora rereads your mail for its suggestions, and travels to your other computers with your settings. A bare address with no name changes nothing. (The Address Book itself isn't changed: edit the contact there to change it everywhere.)

Formatting, plain text, and pictures

The HTML / Plain Text switch on the message window's toolbar chooses how the message is sent, any time before you send it. The half in use is filled in. With the keyboard, Tab to it and use the arrow keys.

  • HTML: formatted, with bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, highlight, lists, links, and pictures in the text. Each message goes with a plain-text copy for mail programs that show only text.
  • Plain Text: words only, which every mail program shows the same way. The formatting buttons rest while it's chosen.

Formatting text: choose the words, then use the toolbar's buttons, the keys, or the right-click menu:

BoldCtrl+B
ItalicCtrl+I
UnderlineCtrl+U
StrikethroughCtrl+Shift+X
HighlightCtrl+Shift+H (yellow; the same again takes it off)

Each button shows whether its formatting is on where the cursor is. Right-click in the text (or press Shift+F10 or the Menu key) for Cut, Copy, Paste, every formatting above, Bulleted and Numbered List, Make Hyperlink…, Remove Formatting, Select All, and Check Spelling. In a plain-text message the formatting items are grayed, with Switch to HTML to format… below them.

Switching to plain text loses the formatting, so Eudora warns first. It lists exactly what would go (bold, italic, or underlined text; fonts, sizes, and colors; lists; tables; pictures in the text; clickable links) and lets you keep HTML. Quoted parts become "> " lines, and each link's address is kept in the text after its words. Switching back to HTML doesn't bring the lost formatting back. A message with nothing but words switches without asking.

A reply to a plain-text message starts as plain text, as does a reply quoted classic Eudora's way.

Pasting a picture (a screenshot, say): in an HTML message it goes in where the cursor is. In a plain-text message Eudora asks whether to switch to HTML and paste it, or attach it as a file. Pictures in the text are sent as parts of the message, which every mail program shows.

Priority marks the message highest to lowest for the people who receive it.

Attaching files

Choose Message ▸ Attach File… (Ctrl+H) or drag files into the message window. Each attached file is listed; × takes it off. Files pasted into the window are attached too.

Signatures

Tools ▸ Signatures… keeps as many named signatures as you like, with formatting. Choose which one each From address starts with, and one for any other address.

A new message ends with the signature; a reply or forward has it right under where you type, above the original. Changing From swaps the signature, unless you edited it. The message window's Signature menu picks another or none. Signatures travel to your other computers with sync.

Checking spelling

Edit ▸ Check Spelling (Ctrl+6) or the Spelling button checks the message with your computer's own spell checker, in your language. For each word: Change, Ignore, Ignore All, or Add (to your computer's dictionary, which other programs share). The quoted original and the signature are skipped.

Dictation

The 🎤 button in the message window starts your computer's voice typing where the cursor is: on Windows, Windows' voice typing; on the Mac, the Mac's dictation. Speak, and the words appear in the message.

Drafts and autosave

While you write, Eudora saves the message to Drafts every 20 seconds, silently, so nothing is lost if the computer or Eudora stops. File ▸ Save Draft (Ctrl+S) saves at once.

File ▸ Drafts… lists your drafts; open one to carry on. Closing a message asks whether to keep it as a draft; Don't save removes the autosaved copy too.

Send Later and the Out mailbox

In the message window choose Send Later… (Message ▸ Change ▸ Queuing… in Classic mode) and pick a date and time, or In an hour or Tomorrow 8 AM. The message waits in the Out mailbox and is sent by itself when its time comes, while Eudora is open. If Eudora was closed at that time, it goes as soon as Eudora starts.

Mailbox ▸ Out (Ctrl+0) lists what waits: Change makes it a draft again; Send now sends it. File ▸ Send Queued Messages (Ctrl+T) sends everything waiting.

A message is never sent twice: if sending was cut off partway, Eudora says so and leaves it for you to check.

Printing

File ▸ Print… (Ctrl+P) prints the message you're reading, with its From, To, Date, and Subject lines, through your computer's print window. Messages always print in light colors.
